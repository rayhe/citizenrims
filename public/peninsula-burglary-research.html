Research brief · September 21, 2026

Residential burglary on the Mid-Peninsula

A 2024–September 2026 evidence review of Atherton, Menlo Park, Palo Alto, and the unincorporated Menlo Oaks corridor — what happened, what officials discussed, and where the record still has gaps.

Bottom line The risk has not disappeared. Reported levels differ by jurisdiction, but the repeated pattern is consistent: early-evening rear entry, fast access to primary bedrooms, and crews moving across city lines.

The recurring method

Across the corridor, the most consistent pattern is a rear glass door or window breached in the early evening, followed by a rapid search of the primary bedroom for portable valuables.

  1. Approach from side or rear
  2. Smash glass or exploit an unlocked opening
  3. Move to the primary bedroom
  4. Take jewelry, cash, coins, safes, bags, or electronics
02

The numbers, without false equivalence

Available series use different definitions. Atherton’s annual report counts all burglaries; Menlo Park reports home burglaries; Palo Alto’s FBI UCR series counts total burglaries and labels 2025 partial. The chart keeps those series separate.

03

What the record shows by jurisdiction

Atherton

2–3 / monthaverage investigations; 10-year average 27/year

The town’s 2024 annual report records 30 burglaries, 10 more than 2023, with $1.7 million in losses across the two years. A separate 2023 residential deck reports 17 that year, versus 20 in the annual report — an unresolved likely-definition gap.

Incidents & pattern

  • Jan. 11, 2025: two Stevick Drive homes hit around 7 p.m.; rear glass doors broken; about $20,000 in jewelry and coins taken from one.
  • Apr. 26, 2025: Isabella Avenue rear-door window removed; entry between 5 and 9 p.m.; fourth reported home burglary of 2025.
  • Oct. 25–27, 2025: three smash-and-glass attempts on Faxon Road, Mulberry Lane, and Faxon Forest Road; no arrests reported.

Response

  • 47 ALPR cameras: 21 town-leased plus 26 resident-funded.
  • 2024 bait-house program with 50+ volunteer homes; GPS tags cost $565 plus $30/year each.
  • $10,000 reward announced Apr. 23, 2025 for the two January cases, the first use of the county pilot.

Sources: town recap, 2024 annual report, 2023 community deck, 2021 ALPR report, Palo Alto Online, and Daily Post.

Menlo Park

32 / 35planned fixed ALPR cameras operating by Dec. 31, 2025

After rejecting Flock on privacy grounds in September 2023, the council reversed course following Sharon Heights burglaries. Home burglaries held nearly flat at 57 in 2024 and 59 in 2025; a January–February 2026 spike later returned to typical levels.

Incidents & pressure

  • June 16, 2024: suspected burglar arrested at a residential construction site; an alleged collaborator escaped.
  • A resident petition in early 2026 claimed 25+ west-side homes hit in a year, including one occupied home invasion. Petitioner claims
  • Public comments pushed safety into the council’s FY2026–27 priority discussion.

Response & oversight

  • 30-day Flock retention; 30 of 35 cameras live July 2, 2025 and 32 by Dec. 31.
  • Out-of-state agencies ran tens of thousands of searches for an unknown period, contrary to California law; disclosed after press inquiry.
  • Chief rejected a Sharon Heights substation as not cost-effective; favored patrols, coordination, home inspections, watch blocks, and a camera registry.

Sources: 2023 council coverage, Q2 2025 PD report, Q4 2025 PD report, Almanac investigation, and May 2026 Daily Post.

Palo Alto

30Flock cameras; roughly $93,500 per year

Palo Alto expanded from 20 cameras approved in 2023 to 30 in 2024. The chief reported a 13% year-over-year reduction in residential burglaries around June 2026, while the 2025 FBI series shows 184 total burglaries for a partial year and cannot be compared as a residential annual total.

Incidents & trend

  • 19 homes hit in about a month in late 2025; 16 south of Oregon Expressway.
  • Nov. 10: three homes in one hour, two occupied; backyard glass doors were breached.
  • Designer handbags, jewelry, and cash were primary targets; no arrests reported in the spree.

Response & controversy

  • Flock was key evidence in 130+ cases since 2024, according to the chief.
  • Two suspects tied to dozens of regional residential burglaries were identified and arrested using multi-jurisdiction Flock data.
  • June 1, 2026 council study session examined Flock’s undisclosed “Nationwide Lookup”; no formal action, cameras remained.

Sources: June 1, 2026 ALPR staff report, Palo Alto Online council coverage, Daily Post council coverage, and south Palo Alto spree report.

Unincorporated county / Menlo Oaks

−23%residential + commercial burglary, YTD Sept. 2024 vs. 2023

The Sheriff’s Office reported a broad year-to-date decline in 2024, but residents in Menlo Oaks described a localized gap: no dedicated patrol, attempted burglaries, and 5–15-minute response times. The Sheriff’s Office said the area received regular patrols and timely responses.

Local picture

  • Historically about 3–4 burglaries per year; six between Nov. 2023 and Feb. 2025.
  • Sheriff count: three home burglaries in 2024 and two in 2025 year-to-date as of Oct. 2.
  • 200+ families funded guards four nights a week plus holidays; 100+ residents joined the security chat.

County response

  • $10,000 reward pilot followed 90+ burglaries in five months across unincorporated areas and nearby cities.
  • Sheriff cited patrols, public-safety forums, and ALPR in the 2024 decline.
  • County supervisors reportedly approved up to $3.3 million for an 18-month Peregrine data platform in January 2026. Unofficial summary

Sources: Sheriff’s Sept. 30, 2024 release, Daily Post on Menlo Oaks, and reward-pilot coverage. Peregrine detail comes from a citizenportal.ai index summary; no source URL was confirmed in the report.

A surge prompted the concept in February; by May 28, 50+ homes had volunteered for GPS-tagged valuables. Atherton had recorded 15 residential burglaries year-to-date by May 28.

Construction-site arrest in Menlo Park

A suspected burglar was arrested at a residential construction site; an alleged collaborator escaped.

Sheriff reports a broad decline

Residential and commercial burglary down 23% year-to-date; larceny down 12%; stolen property down 38%, all versus the same 2023 period.

December 2, 2024

Palo Alto expands Flock

Council approves 10 more cameras, bringing the planned total to 30 under a five-year contract through December 2029.

Two Stevick Drive homes hit

Rear glass doors broken around 7 p.m.; roughly $20,000 in jewelry and gold and silver coins stolen from one home.

Faxon Road, Mulberry Lane, and Faxon Forest Road; glass was smashed, no arrests reported.

Spike drives policy debate

07

What this means for Menlo Oaks

Menlo Oaks lies in the Sheriff’s jurisdiction but directly borders two active burglary-suppression systems. The evidence supports practical actions now and a political question about patrol capacity.

01

Risk is moderate, not imaginary

The historic baseline is about three to four burglaries a year, with six between Nov. 2023 and Feb. 2025. Attempted burglaries appear to drive more concern than completed-case statistics capture.

02

Design for the repeated entry pattern

Prioritize rear glass protection, locked side gates, exterior lighting and motion detection, a monitored alarm, and a safe bolted to structure. Keep high-value portable items out of the primary bedroom.

03

Make street cameras investigative

Resident-funded cameras are already in place. A formal registry or footage-sharing path with the Sheriff could make them more useful across jurisdiction lines, following the model Menlo Park’s chief proposed.

04

Use the county lever

The Mueller reward pilot covers unincorporated-area burglaries. The longer-term ask is dedicated patrol capacity and clear performance reporting for unincorporated neighborhoods.

05

Watch winter and evening hours

Atherton’s January 2025 cases and Menlo Park’s Jan.–Feb. 2026 spike, plus repeated 5–10 p.m. entry windows, support extra attention during winter evenings.

06

Demand cross-border accountability

The reported Peregrine platform could improve regional case linkage, but its official approval record, access rules, and measured outcomes remain open questions in this review.

08

Corrections, caveats, and open questions

  • No verified Chilean-crew link to the January 2025 Atherton cases. The documented link belongs to the 2021–2022 Atherton wave. Treat the newer attribution as a conflation.
  • Atherton’s 2025 year-end count was not found. The available record has four reported home burglaries by Apr. 27 plus three October attempts.
  • Atherton’s 2023 figures conflict. The community deck reports 17 residential burglaries; the 2024 annual report reports 20 burglaries. Likely different definitions, not resolved.
  • Menlo Park’s May 12, 2026 official minutes were not located. Findings come from press coverage of the chief’s report.
  • The March 21, 2026 Menlo Park workshop is supported only by an AI-generated summary. It is not treated as an official record.
  • County Legistar file 24-923 could not be fetched. Its possible connection to the reward pilot remains unverified.
  • No arrests were reported for the October 2025 Atherton spree or November 2025 Palo Alto spree. This review did locate a June 2024 Menlo Park arrest and a March 2025 Atherton attempted-burglary arrest.
